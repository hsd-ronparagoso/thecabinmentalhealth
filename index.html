03 — Therapeutic Approaches

Which Therapies Can Help With Depression?

Therapies that can help with depression range from structured, skills-focused approaches to more personal or peer-based formats. A program may draw on one or several together, depending on individual circumstances.

01

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

CBT is a structured therapeutic approach that examines how thoughts, feelings, and behaviors influence one another. Sessions help a person recognize unhelpful patterns, build alternative ways of thinking, and practice coping strategies for difficult situations.

02

Individual Therapy

Individual therapy provides one-to-one sessions with a qualified therapist or mental health professional. Sessions offer a private setting to work through personal experiences, emotional difficulties, and relationship challenges, shaped around each person's specific treatment goals.

03

Group Therapy

Group therapy involves structured sessions with a qualified therapist or mental health professional and a small group of participants. Sessions create space to build coping skills, share experiences, and practice communication, with peer support that individual therapy alone cannot offer.

04 — Co-Occurring Conditions

What Other Mental Health Conditions Can Be Addressed?

Conditions that can be addressed alongside depression range from anxiety to trauma-related concerns and the effects of prolonged stress, each requiring its own therapeutic focus.

01

Anxiety

Anxiety involves persistent fear, worry, or apprehension that can affect sleep, concentration, relationships, and everyday routine. A treatment program typically addresses both sets of symptoms together rather than treating each in isolation when it occurs alongside depression.

02

Trauma and PTSD

Trauma can leave a lasting psychological impact, and for some patients this develops into post-traumatic stress disorder following a traumatic event. Treatment needs to account for both the depressive symptoms and the effects of the trauma itself when trauma-related symptoms occur alongside depression.

03

Burnout and Stress

Prolonged or excessive stress can lead to emotional exhaustion, reduced motivation, difficulty concentrating, and disruption to everyday functioning. Burnout and depression aren't the same condition, though symptoms can overlap, so professional assessment helps distinguish between them and identify the right kind of support.

05 — Conditions We Treat

What Mental Health Conditions do we Cover in Thailand?

Mental health conditions treated across Thailand span a wide range of emotional, psychological, and behavioral difficulties. Treatment settings range from public hospitals to private clinics and specialized rehabilitation centers, depending on severity.

Anxiety

Anxiety involves persistent worry, nervousness, or fear that can interfere with daily functioning. Physical symptoms such as rapid heartbeat and restlessness frequently accompany the emotional distress.

Depression

Depression involves persistent low mood, loss of interest, and reduced energy that can affect motivation and daily activity. Symptoms often build gradually and can persist for weeks or months without treatment.

Trauma and PTSD

Trauma and PTSD develop after a distressing or life-threatening event that continues to affect emotional and psychological functioning. Flashbacks, hypervigilance, and avoidance behaviors commonly accompany this condition.

Burnout

Burnout results from prolonged stress, often work-related, leading to emotional exhaustion and reduced motivation. Cynicism and detachment from responsibilities frequently accompany this state.

Mood Disorders

Mood disorders involve significant shifts in emotional state, including depressive and manic episodes in some cases. These shifts can affect energy, sleep, and daily decision-making.

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

Obsessive-compulsive disorder involves intrusive thoughts paired with repetitive behaviors aimed at reducing distress. These patterns can consume significant time and interfere with daily responsibilities.

Eating Disorders

Eating disorders involve a disrupted relationship with food, weight, or body image that affects physical and emotional health. These conditions require specialized treatment addressing both psychological and physical dimensions.

Substance Use Disorders

Substance use disorders involve compulsive use of drugs or alcohol despite clear harm to health and daily functioning. Treatment often addresses both the substance use and any underlying mental health conditions.

When Should You Seek Additional Support?

Contacting a licensed mental health professional may be worthwhile if you notice: depression symptoms returning or intensifying, growing distance from family and friends, an ongoing sense of hopelessness, shifts in sleep or appetite, trouble keeping up with daily obligations, or struggling to follow through with a recovery plan.

Immediate danger or a mental health crisis calls for urgent contact with local emergency services or the closest emergency facility.
